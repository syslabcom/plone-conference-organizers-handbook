# Making contact — operator cheat sheet

The row in `outreach-targets.csv` is the operational source of truth. The workbook is the derived operator view: filter there, record in the CSV. `REPORT.md` supplies rationale, but its portfolio counts are stale.

## The process

1. **Filter your territory in the workbook.** In `outreach-targets.xlsx`, filter `country_region` (column E) to your territory — not NL for the German rows — and `tier` (column V) to Tier 1. Check whether any institution appears twice (two units of the same city or university); if so, treat them as one coordinated story, not two independent contacts.
2. **Pick one target and verify its data at source.** Re-check the organisation-owned contact route, the current activity evidence, the matching published session or programme route, the public schedule, and the ticket price and path. A saved row is not evidence a fact is still current.
3. **Pick one thing to offer.** Business Track or the regular developer conference — never both in one message. The product facts are below.
4. **Pick one thing to ask.** One ask only: a calendar or newsletter placement, consider buying a ticket, sponsorship, an introduction, a routing to the right colleague. For multipliers the ask is one named action — never member access.
5. **Write the relevance sentence and create the pitch file.** One sentence: why this is relevant to one named reader, grounded in their verified current work. Create the pitch file from `kit/PITCH_TEMPLATE.md` — one reader, one product, one ask. Native review before external use for Dutch, German or French copy.
6. **Call them; mail only if a call is not possible.** Use the pitch's call opening. Pause and listen before giving programme detail.
7. **After the call, send the handout only if they ask for details.** Only the selected product's PDF from `kit/handouts/`; pair the technical one with one current published session; re-check its facts before attaching. Use `kit/FOLLOW_UP.md` for the follow-up shape.
8. **Record the outcome in the CSV.** Status, date, mechanism, the exact answer, the next action, and any objection or suppression. The CSV is the only tracker — do not build a second one.

## Boundaries

This is operating guidance, not legal advice.

- **Netherlands and Germany are consent-first.** Public availability of an address is not consent. A warm introducer first asks whether contact is welcome. One unanswered message does not permit a follow-up sequence.
- **Belgium has a narrow legal-person exception.** It applies only where an impersonal legal-person inbox is factually suitable for the business-directed message. Named employee addresses and person-linked functional inboxes remain consent-first.
- In every country: identify the sender and purpose, use organisation-owned routes, never infer personal addresses, never request member or subscriber lists, provide a simple way to decline, and honour suppression.

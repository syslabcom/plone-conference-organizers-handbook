## On-site Roles — Plone Conference 2026

Written 13 August 2026. These are day-in-the-life stories, not job descriptions.

Each file walks one person through their days from the moment they wake up to the moment they hand over. The purpose is twofold: the designated person can picture the day and see what is expected, and we can read the stories against each other to find the gaps — a task nobody owns, a room nobody unlocks, a box nobody carries.

Read your own file, then read the two roles you hand off to. Most failures on site happen at a handover, not inside a role.

### These names are responsibles, not workers

The nine names above **manage**. They do not execute alone, and no story assumes they do. Each of them coordinates a small number of community helpers who do the routine work, and each story now carries a **Your helpers** section stating how many, when, and what they do.

The clearest case is the Conference Manager. Ruben does not stand in a room holding a timer — if he does, three other rooms are unattended and nobody is watching the whole. He builds and runs the roster of the people who do stand in the rooms. Same principle everywhere: the responsible's hands stay empty so their attention can move.

Two rules that apply to every helper slot:

**Half-day shifts, rotated.** Helpers are paying attendees. Somebody who hosts rooms for three full days sees no talks and does not come back next year. The one exception is recording operators, where skill retention across the three days genuinely beats fairness — those people get compensated differently, by choosing which room they operate.

**Confirmed by name the evening before.** Published roster, then a message to each person. Assumption is how a room ends up without a host.

### One thing this roster still does not solve

**Ruben's Thursday runs 09:00 to 01:00, and Friday starts at 09:00.** Conference Manager for three days plus Party Manager on Thursday night is one hat too many on the one night it matters. The mitigation is in story 06: a **door deputy** who owns the entrance, the guest list and the coins outright, so Ruben keeps only the room, the venue and the DJ. Ruben also owns Marketing and Print in the last-phase plan and is the assignee on most open issues, so this is a concentration risk rather than just a long day.

### Settled

- **The party is Thursday 24 September**, 19:00–01:00 at the Muziekgieterij, as the signed contract states. Friday night stays empty. Every planning document now says so; the website says nothing about the party at all, which is WD-08 in `planning/website-defects.md`.
- **We record all three rooms.** One recent GoPro is available; the other two rooms have no camera yet, so kit for two more positions has to be bought. We are not using a house AV contractor — the buying list in story 07 covers all three rooms.
- **Remote presentations are best-effort, not promised.** One speaker asked whether it is possible in theory. If it can run on the kit we have at no extra cost we will try; otherwise we ask for a prerecorded talk. Story 07 covers the detail.
- **No door sales.** Tickets are bought online; once payment is confirmed, a badge is filled in by hand at the desk.
- **A bus for the party is being organised** — up to 150 people to the Muziekgieterij and back after 01:00. Price and booking deadline are question 39 on the venue call.
